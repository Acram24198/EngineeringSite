01 / Project Briefing

A shorter route, a stronger connection.

Two coastal areas currently separated by a roughly 30-minute drive could be connected by a new two-mile bridge. The hypothetical project aims to improve access, encourage tourism, and create community waterfront amenities.

01 / ACCESS

Improve Connectivity

Develop a direct crossing between the two areas. Travel-time savings will depend on the final alignment, posted speed, and bridge operating schedule.

02 / NAVIGATION

Keep the Channel Open

A rotating swing span would open when needed to accommodate vessels using the narrow tidal channel.

03 / COMMUNITY

Support Waterfront Access

Include a public boat ramp, dock, and facilities as part of the overall coastal development concept.

02 / Structural Concept

A fixed crossing with a movable center.

The overall bridge would span approximately 10,560 feet. Most of the crossing would remain fixed, while a shorter swing span at the navigable channel could rotate to permit marine traffic.

03 / Site & Environment

Designing for a changing coastline.

At low tide, much of the wetland is exposed. At higher tides, water covers more of the site while the narrow channel remains navigable. The bridge concept must account for both conditions.

01 / WETLANDS

Minimize Disturbance

Investigate longer spans and fewer in-wetland supports to reduce the potential footprint on sensitive habitat and tidal flow.

02 / FOUNDATIONS

Evaluate Ground Conditions

Soft coastal soils may require deep foundations. Site surveys and geotechnical investigations would be necessary for a real project.

03 / WATERFRONT

Plan Public Facilities

Consider boat-ramp slope across tidal levels, dock access, parking, pedestrian circulation, and restrooms.
